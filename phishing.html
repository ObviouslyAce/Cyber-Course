Phishing Scams

Phishing is like a digital con artist trying to trick you into giving away sensitive information. Scammers impersonate trusted entities to steal your personal data, financial information, or account credentials.

Recognizing Phishing

Phishing attempts can come through multiple channels, making them particularly dangerous:

  • Email phishing: Deceptive emails claiming to be from legitimate organizations
  • Spear phishing: Targeted attacks using personal information about you
  • Smishing: SMS text message phishing
  • Vishing: Voice call phishing

For example, you might receive an urgent email appearing to be from your bank asking you to "verify your account" by clicking a link.

Typical Signs

Look out for these red flags that often indicate a phishing attempt:

  • Generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your name
  • Poor grammar, spelling errors, or unusual phrasing
  • Requests for personal information or credentials
  • Suspicious or mismatched URLs (hover before clicking)
  • Creating a false sense of urgency or threat

Attackers frequently use urgency tactics like "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours unless you verify your information" to rush you into making mistakes.

Prevention Methods

Protect yourself from phishing attacks with these proven methods:

  • Never click suspicious links—type the official website address directly in your browser
  • Enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts
  • Verify requests for information by contacting the company directly using official contact information
  • Keep software, browsers, and security tools updated
  • Use anti-phishing features in your email service and security software

Report Phishing Attempts

If you've received a suspicious email or message, help protect others by reporting it:

  • Forward phishing emails to phishing-report@ftc.gov
  • Report to your organization's IT security team
  • Report via the impersonated company's official channels
