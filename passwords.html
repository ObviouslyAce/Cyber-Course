Password Strength

A strong password is like a good lock on your front door—the stronger the lock, the harder it is for someone to break in. Similarly, the stronger your password, the more secure your account is.

Length

The longer your password, the more difficult it is to guess. Think of it like a lock with more pins—the more pins, the harder it is to pick. A strong password is generally at least 12–16 characters long and uses a variety of characters, such as:

  • Uppercase letters (A–Z)
  • Lowercase letters (a–z)
  • Numbers (0–9)
  • Special characters (!, @, #, $, %)

For example, a good password might be iL0vE$3cur3!, as it combines letters, numbers, and symbols.

Unpredictability

Avoid predictable patterns like:

  • "abc123" or "qwerty"
  • Repeated characters (e.g., "aaaaaa")
  • Sequences (e.g., "12345" or "abcdef")

Hackers use automated tools to quickly guess common passwords, so the more unusual and random your password is, the better.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Even with a strong password, it’s wise to enable two-factor authentication (2FA). This adds an extra layer of security by requiring a second piece of information—like a secret code—in addition to your password.
